The shape of a good page

A specimen of prose, hierarchy, and the small details between them.

Author

The Elegant notebook

Published

October 16, 2026

A comfortable measure

The first obligation of a page is to be read. A comfortable line, a generous margin, and an unhurried rhythm do much of the work. The details become noticeable through their consistency: the distance above a heading, the weight of a rule, the way a link sits inside a sentence.

This paragraph contains emphasis, a stronger point, and a link to the code specimen. A reference to library.find() takes its place in the sentence without changing the rhythm. Numbers such as 0123456789 and punctuation — dashes, “quotation marks,” and parentheses — should feel at home together.

A smaller thought

The hierarchy comes from a few deliberate changes in size and weight. A heading should help a reader skim the page and then settle back into the text.

One useful detail

Subheadings stay clear at a smaller scale. They introduce a brief explanation, an exception, or the next step in an example.

Lists and quotations

  1. Start with something concrete: a question, a small example, or a result.
  2. Explain enough for the reader to make sense of it.
  3. Leave a useful route to the next idea.
  • A list can be brief.
  • A longer entry wraps naturally, with its continuation aligned to its own text. The indentation keeps the page easy to scan.
  • Nested detail should earn its place.

Read a little, think a little, and write something worth returning to. A notebook grows through attention, one useful page at a time.
